What Is New

FATEBLADE began as a browser game. This is the ground-up rebuild in the Godot engine: the same rules, proven identical move for move, with everything a player sees and hears made new.

  • A table that feels alive. Cards tilt under the mouse and slam onto the board. Minions wind up and lunge, ranged bodies loose bolts, edicts burn away, and the killing blow lands in slow motion.
  • An opponent that taught itself. The Impossible AI is a neural network trained by playing itself every night. It plans whole turns ahead, finds lethal when it exists, and in testing beats the Hard AI about 69% of the time.
  • An original orchestral score. Composed for the game around one theme. The match music has two layers, and the battle layer rises as the board fills and Sovereigns bleed.
  • Every card has a voice. A card's sound is built from its tribe, its keywords and what it does. All 56 legendaries arrive with their own motif, and each realm has a musical signature.
  • Printed cards. Cast-metal frames by rarity, names that always fit their ribbon, and rules text that never spills.
  • Ways to play. Skirmish against four AI levels, the eight-chapter campaign The Broken Oath, a guided tutorial, a deck builder, hotseat for two, and WATCH, where two AIs play with commentary.

How It's Made

FATEBLADE is a one-person project, built with an AI-assisted development workflow and a heavy bias toward measuring instead of guessing.

  • The Godot engine is checked against the original game on every change: 139,554 automated checks, down to the random number generator, so both versions play out identically
  • The Impossible AI trains itself overnight; a new version only replaces the champion when it wins a statistically significant head-to-head
  • Every screen is exercised by scripted play-throughs, and AI-versus-AI soak runs hunt for matches that stall
  • Card frames, interface art, sound effects and the entire score are generated by scripts in the repository, so the whole game can be rebuilt from source

This is a prototype. Expect rough edges, and tell me where you find them.
